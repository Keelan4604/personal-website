About

Requirements on one side, hardware on the other.

I like the part of engineering where the two meet: writing the requirement, building the test, running the vehicle, and finding out where the model and the hardware disagree.

That has looked like bench-testing UAV propellers at 10,000 RPM against CFD predictions, flying the eVTOL my capstone team designed and built, restoring more than sixty jet skis and boats from teardown through functional test, and designing filtration components to Caterpillar and Komatsu fitment guidelines for my family's business.

Now I'm at the University of Michigan for an MS in Aerospace Engineering with a Space Systems focus. My main project is a six-person team designing a standardized lunar cargo module for Northrop Grumman through Michigan's Multidisciplinary Design Program, where I own the requirements set, the verification methods, and the mass and power budgets.

Outside of that I fix things with engines, run a 3D printer, and keep a small home lab of self-hosted services running.

Northrop Grumman sponsored, Michigan MDPSep 2026 - present

CAT-LIT lunar cargo module, systems engineer

Six-person team designing a standardized cargo module that lands 8,000 kg on the lunar surface with a dry mass under 2,000 kg, compatible with both Starship and Blue Moon and scalable across 5 m, 7 m and 8 m fairings. Think shipping container for the Moon: the value is the standardized interface, and the hard problem is getting cargo from a 30 m airlock down to the regolith and where it needs to go.

  • Own the Level 1 through Level 4 requirements set and the verification method for every requirement, following the NASA Systems Engineering Handbook (SP-2016-6105).
  • Maintain the vehicle mass and power budgets across pressurized, unpressurized, powered and unpowered cargo variants.
  • Mentored by Dr. Anthony DeCicco, Senior Mission Architect, and Andrew Kwas, Executive NG Fellow. Presenting to NG leadership in December 2026.

UAV propeller thrust vs. acoustics

Designed, SLA 3D-printed and bench-tested four UAV propeller geometries at 10,000 RPM to map thrust against acoustic signature for the Vertical Flight Society chapter's research program.

  • Built the test stand with load-cell thrust instrumentation and microphone acoustic profiling, and wrote the run procedures so every geometry was compared the same way.
  • Correlated measured results against CFD and FEA predictions to isolate where the models diverged from hardware.
  • Presented the findings at the URCA Conference 2025.

ORCA fixed-wing eVTOL UAV

A runway-independent reconnaissance UAV that takes off vertically, transitions to efficient forward cruise, and swaps sensors through a modular nose. My team designed, built and flew it.

  • Designed the modular payload nose with standardized mechanical and electrical interfaces, so optical, IR and thermal sensors swap without touching the airframe.
  • Served as team pilot and ran the flight test campaign: phased hover trials, then vertical-to-forward transition trials validating stability, control response and endurance.
  • Modeled the propulsion tilt-path dynamics and servo actuation sequencing in MATLAB/Simulink and validated the power budget against measured draw.

Solid and liquid rocket propulsion system design

Two end-to-end propulsion system designs from mission delta-V down to hardware sizing, worked in Python and MATLAB with a full report for each.

  • Solid motor for a 5,000 kg payload orbit raise: propellant mass from the two-burn Hohmann delta-V, motor case and burst pressure at a 5.17 MPa average chamber pressure, thrust skirt, insulation, nozzle throat from c* and an 80 s burn, and the web and port ballistics.
  • Liquid H2/LOX stage moving a 50,000 kg payload from GEO to lunar orbit at 450 s Isp, with tank sizing from the 5.5 mixture ratio and propellant densities.
